A New York court has ordered the founders of crypto trading company NovaTech to pay $1,367,023,585, about $1.37 billion, for running what the state calls a crypto Ponzi scheme that targeted Haitian communities. New York Attorney General Letitia James [announced the judgment](L1) on Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026, calling it "historic."

The founders, Cynthia and Eddy Petion, never showed up to defend themselves. The state says they fled the country, which means the hard part, actually collecting the money, is still ahead.

!takeaways
- A New York judge ordered Cynthia and Eddy Petion and three NovaTech companies to pay $1,367,023,585, jointly.
- That total is $1,134,549,691 in disgorgement (giving back money from the fraud) plus $232,473,894 in interest at 9% a year.
- Investors deposited more than $1 billion in crypto, but less than $26 million was ever traded, according to the state's complaint.
- The victims include at least 11,000 New Yorkers. Four promoters have already agreed to pay nearly $6 million.
- The Petions fled the country, so it is unclear how much of the judgment can be recovered.

!figure:fact

## What NovaTech promised

NovaTech presented itself as a crypto and foreign exchange trading platform that could deliver high, steady returns. According to the lawsuit New York filed in June 2024, the company and an earlier crypto mining scheme, AWS Mining, recruited investors through prayer groups, social media and WhatsApp group chats, especially among Haitian immigrants.

In reality, the state says, very little trading happened. Investors deposited more than $1 billion worth of crypto, but less than $26 million went to trading, [The Block](L4) reported, citing the complaint. Earlier investors were paid with money from newer investors. That is the classic definition of a Ponzi scheme, and it collapses once new deposits slow down.

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) brought its own case in August 2024, alleging that NovaTech raised more than $650 million from more than 200,000 investors worldwide, [according to the SEC](L3).

## What the court ordered

The court found the Petions liable by default in August 2025 after they failed to respond, and banned them from the securities, commodities and crypto industries. The new [order and judgment](L2), filed on Nov. 24, 2026, puts a dollar figure on it. The Petions and the companies Nova Tech Ltd, NovaTech Advisors and NovaPay owe $1,134,549,691 in disgorgement plus $232,473,894 in interest, counted at 9% a year from June 1, 2023, to Sept. 10, 2025.

The judgment also orders AWS Mining to pay $1,455,666 and Cynthia Petion to pay a further $4,694,712 tied to her promotion of AWS Mining.

## Can victims get their money back?

A court judgment is a right to collect, not a refund. The attorney general says the couple fled the country, and no claims process for victims has been announced. Money that moved to regulated exchanges can sometimes be frozen by court order. Crypto held in wallets the Petions control is much harder to reach.

So far, the state has secured nearly $6 million from four promoters, Martin Zizi, Frantz Ciceron, Ricardo Roy Sr. and James Corbett, who were also banned from the industry. That is less than 1% of the new judgment.

## What it means for beginners

Affinity scams spread through trust: a pastor, a cousin, a group chat full of people who look like you. Be very careful with any crypto "trading platform" that promises steady high returns, pays bonuses for recruiting friends, or asks you to send crypto to its own wallet. Real investments go up and down.

If you want to own bitcoin, the plainer route is to buy a small amount yourself on a regulated U.S. exchange such as [Coinbase](REF), where the asset sits in your own account. Bitcoin is volatile, so only invest what you can afford to lose. This is not financial advice.

## What to watch next

- Whether New York sets up a claims process for NovaTech victims.
- Any assets traced to the Petions and frozen by courts.
- The SEC's separate NovaTech case, which is still going on in federal court in Florida.
