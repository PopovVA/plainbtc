Malaysia is about to get its first bitcoin exchange-traded fund. Halogen Capital issued the prospectus for the Halogen Shariah Bitcoin ETF on Friday, Nov. 27, and the fund is scheduled to start trading on the Main Market of Bursa Malaysia, the country's stock exchange, on Wednesday, Dec. 2. Halogen describes it as the world's first Shariah-compliant bitcoin ETF and the first digital-currency ETF on Bursa Malaysia, according to [EconoTimes](L3).

The Securities Commission Malaysia approved the fund's establishment and listing on Oct. 26, and Bursa Malaysia approved the admission of up to 1 billion units on Nov. 16, the prospectus says. Halogen's [website](L1) lists two trading counters: BTCETF-MYR in Malaysian ringgit and BTCETF-USD in U.S. dollars.

!takeaways
- Listing on Bursa Malaysia's Main Market is scheduled for Dec. 2, 2026, after an initial subscription on Nov. 30 and allotment on Dec. 1. The dates can still change.
- At least 95% of the fund's net assets go into physical spot bitcoin. The rest can sit in Islamic money-market instruments, Islamic deposits or Islamic funds to help with liquidity.
- The prospectus allows up to 1 billion units at an initial price of US$1.00 each. The management fee is up to 0.60% of net asset value a year.
- Amanie Advisors is the Shariah adviser, Universal Trustee (Malaysia) is the trustee, and BitGo holds the bitcoin as sub-custodian.

## How the fund works

An ETF is a fund that trades on a stock exchange like a share. Instead of buying bitcoin on a crypto exchange and keeping it in a wallet, an investor buys units of the fund through an ordinary brokerage account, and the fund holds the coins. Halogen's ETF aims to closely track the FTSE Bitcoin Index, according to the [prospectus](L2).

Units will trade in board lots of 100, and investors must sell in the same currency they used to buy, Malaysian outlet DagangNews reported, as cited by [CoinMarketCap](L4). The initial subscription on Nov. 30 is a private placement with the participating dealer, CGS International Securities Malaysia. Other investors can buy through licensed brokers once the units list.

If all 1 billion units were sold at US$1, the fund would raise US$1 billion, or about 4.12 billion ringgit. The prospectus does not say how many units will be created at the start, CoinMarketCap noted.

## What makes it Shariah-compliant

Islamic finance follows Shariah principles, which among other things forbid earning interest. That is why the fund's small cash portion is limited to Islamic instruments instead of ordinary interest-paying deposits. Amanie Advisors, the fund's Shariah adviser, certified the fund as Shariah-compliant, and the fund will not pay out income, according to reports cited by CoinMarketCap.

Halogen already runs a Shariah bitcoin fund that does not trade on an exchange. Its website says the Halogen Shariah Bitcoin Fund started on Aug. 23, 2023. The ETF puts the same idea on the stock exchange, where investors can trade it during market hours like any listed share.

## What it costs and the risks

According to [EconoTimes](L3), which reviewed the prospectus, the yearly charges include a management fee of up to 0.60% of net asset value, a trustee fee of up to 0.05% (not counting custody fees) and a sub-custodian fee of up to 0.15%. Buyers and sellers also pay brokerage, a Bursa clearing fee of 0.03% of the trade value (capped at 1,000 ringgit) and stamp duty.

The prospectus lists bitcoin's price swings and currency risk among the main risks. Units can also trade above or below the value of the bitcoin the fund holds, partly because Bursa Malaysia has limited trading hours while bitcoin trades around the clock.

For comparison, Switzerland's new spot bitcoin fund, which started trading on Nov. 26, targets a total expense ratio of 0.19% a year. Read our story on [Switzerland's first spot bitcoin ETF](/articles/switzerland-first-spot-bitcoin-etf-bbit-six-350-btc-nov-2026).

## Why it matters

The fund adds to a growing list of regulated ways to own bitcoin outside the United States, and it arrives at a weak moment for the market. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs have seen [record withdrawals in November](/articles/bitcoin-etfs-record-4-71-billion-november-outflows-nov-2026), and bitcoin traded near $60,000 on Friday. [Blockonomi](L5) noted that Malaysia's approval comes even as investors pull money from U.S. funds.

The fund is listed in Malaysia and built for investors there. Americans already have spot bitcoin ETFs on U.S. exchanges, and they can also buy bitcoin directly on a regulated U.S. exchange such as [Coinbase](REF). Whichever route you choose, bitcoin is volatile, so only invest what you can afford to lose. This is not financial advice.

## What to watch next

- Whether the fund lists on Dec. 2 as planned, and how much bitcoin it holds after the first subscription.
- Trading volume on the ringgit and dollar counters in the first weeks.
- Whether other Malaysian fund managers bring digital-currency ETFs to Bursa Malaysia.
