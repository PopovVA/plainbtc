===

Bitcoin had a wild night. Late on Friday, Nov. 20, the price broke well below the 2026 low it had set only hours earlier, and at <strong>12:17 a.m. Central time on Saturday</strong> it touched <strong>$55,100</strong> on Kraken and $55,106 on Coinbase, according to PlainBTC's check of public price data from [Kraken](L1) and [Coinbase](L2). On Coinbase, that was the lowest price since September 2024.

Then the selling stopped. By 8:03 a.m. Central time on Saturday, bitcoin had climbed back to $60,883 on both exchanges, about 10.5% above the overnight low, and it was trading near $60,300 a few minutes later. On Friday afternoon we reported that bitcoin had set a [new 2026 low near $57,660](/articles/bitcoin-new-2026-low-57661-strategy-shares-sink-nov-2026). The overnight drop took it about $2,500 lower than that.

!takeaways
- Bitcoin fell to $55,100 on Kraken and $55,106 on Coinbase at 12:17 a.m. Central time on Nov. 21, its lowest price since September 2024.
- About $2.4 billion in leveraged crypto bets were liquidated over 24 hours, more than $1 billion of them in bitcoin, according to CoinGlass data cited by CoinDesk.
- By 8:03 a.m. Central time bitcoin was back at $60,883, a 10.5% rebound from the low. No single piece of news explained the bounce.
- U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost a reported $1.25 billion this week, with BlackRock's Friday figure still missing.

!figure:chart

## How the drop unfolded

For most of Friday evening bitcoin traded near $59,000. Around 9 p.m. Central time it started to slide, and within an hour it was below $56,500 on Kraken. The fall paused near $55,500 and then made one more push lower just after midnight, to the $55,100 low. Within the same hour the price snapped back to about $58,000.

[CoinDesk reported](L3) that crypto exchanges liquidated about $2.40 billion in leveraged futures bets over 24 hours, citing CoinGlass. More than $1 billion of that was in bitcoin and $545 million in ether. The largest single liquidation was a $51.71 million bet that bitcoin would rise. [Bitcoin.com News](L4), also citing CoinGlass, put the total at $2.54 billion by 2:11 a.m. Eastern time, with bets on higher prices making up 89.4% of it.

A liquidation happens when an exchange closes a trader's borrowed position because the trader no longer has enough money in the account to cover losses. In a fast drop, those forced sales push prices down further and trigger even more liquidations.

!figure:fact

## Why markets were selling

The drop did not start inside crypto. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3% on Friday as a selloff in AI and chip stocks spread. [Investing.com reported](L5) that shares of AI developer Anthropic fell more than 14% after its stock market debut, and that bitcoin miners such as Hut 8, Core Scientific, CleanSpark and Bitdeer dropped more than 13%. It also pointed to rising inflation-adjusted U.S. Treasury yields and expectations of more Federal Reserve rate increases, which make cash and bonds more attractive than assets like bitcoin.

Other coins fell hard too. Ether dropped to $1,535.37 on Coinbase overnight, still above its 2026 low of $1,505.75 on Kraken from June 6, and was back near $1,690 on Saturday morning. [CoinDesk said](L6) XRP sank to its lowest price since November 2024. On Kraken, XRP touched $0.85918 before recovering to about $0.92.

## What drove the bounce

There was no big headline behind the rebound. When nearly all the leveraged bets on higher prices have been wiped out, fewer forced sellers remain, and traders who bet on a fall often buy back to lock in profits. That can lift prices quickly. Investing.com noted that market maker Wintermute has said bitcoin would need to get back above $68,000, together with new money flowing into spot ETFs, before a lasting recovery looks convincing. In other words, one strong morning does not mean the decline is over.

## ETF money is still leaving

Big investors have been pulling out all week. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost a net $1.12 billion from Monday to Thursday, according to [Farside Investors](L7), including $582.3 million on Thursday. Friday's reported outflow was another $124.5 million, led by $72.4 million from Fidelity's FBTC, but the figure for BlackRock's IBIT, the largest fund, had not been published yet. That makes about $1.25 billion out for the week so far. Spot ether ETFs lost a reported $221.7 million over the same five days, according to [Farside's ether table](L8).

!figure:etf

The [Crypto Fear & Greed Index](L9) stood at 12 out of 100 on Saturday, which counts as "extreme fear." On Kraken, bitcoin opened November at $77,437, so even after Saturday's bounce it is down about 22% this month.

## What it means for beginners

A drop of nearly $4,000 in a few hours, followed by a jump of almost $6,000, shows how fast crypto can move, especially when many traders use borrowed money. Nobody can reliably say whether $55,100 was the bottom. If you are thinking about buying, many beginners make small purchases spread over weeks or months instead of buying all at once, and some start on a large regulated exchange such as [Coinbase](REF). Avoid leverage, and only invest money you can afford to lose. This is not investment advice.

## What to watch next

- Whether bitcoin holds above $60,000 or slips back toward the overnight low of about $55,100.
- BlackRock's IBIT figure for Friday and next week's ETF flows.
- Stock markets on Monday and Fed comments ahead of the Dec. 8-9 meeting.
