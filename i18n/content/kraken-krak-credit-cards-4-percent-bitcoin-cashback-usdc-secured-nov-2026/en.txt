Kraken wants a spot in your wallet, not just your crypto portfolio. On Tuesday, Nov. 24, Krak, the money app run by Kraken's parent company Payward, opened a waitlist for two U.S. credit cards: the <strong>Krak Credit Card</strong> and the <strong>Krak Secured Card</strong>. Both are Visa Signature cards that promise <strong>up to 4% cashback</strong>, paid in U.S. dollars or in bitcoin, and both are expected to start shipping in <strong>early 2027</strong>, according to a company statement shared with [The Block](L1).

The cards arrive in a tough month for crypto. Bitcoin traded near $60,000 on Tuesday after a [record month of ETF outflows](/articles/bitcoin-etfs-record-4-71-billion-november-outflows-nov-2026), a reminder that a card paying rewards in bitcoin also hands you a small, volatile asset with every purchase.

!takeaways
- Krak opened a U.S. waitlist on Nov. 24, 2026 for a standard credit card and a secured credit card. Shipping is expected in early 2027.
- Both cards offer up to 4% cashback in dollars or bitcoin, credited to your Krak account after a purchase settles, not as points.
- The secured card uses $350 to $5,000 in USDC, a dollar stablecoin, as collateral. That USDC can earn up to 4% APY through Krakpass.
- Both cards require Krakpass, which costs $6.29 a month billed annually, or nothing with more than $3,000 in monthly direct deposits.
- Interest rates (APR) and card fees have not been published yet. The cards will not be available in every U.S. state.

!figure:fact

## Two cards, two kinds of customers

The standard Krak Credit Card is meant for people who already have an established credit history. The Krak Secured Card is aimed at people building or rebuilding credit, including what Krak calls crypto-native customers whose savings sit mostly outside traditional banks. Krak said it does not guarantee that using the card will create or improve a credit score.

A secured card works like a normal credit card, except you put down a deposit that backs your credit limit. With Krak, that deposit is USDC, a stablecoin designed to stay worth $1. Customers can deposit between <strong>$350 and $5,000</strong> in USDC depending on the limit they want, a spokesperson told The Block. The coins stay in the customer's Krak account as collateral and can earn up to 4% APY through Krakpass, while purchases can still earn up to 4% cashback.

Cashback is credited once a purchase settles, in dollars or bitcoin. If you pick bitcoin, the value of that reward will rise and fall with the market after you receive it.

## Who is behind the cards, and what it costs

First Electronic Bank will issue the cards under a license from Visa U.S.A., and Cardless, a fintech that runs branded credit card programs, will handle program management and servicing. Krak already sells a U.S. <strong>debit</strong> card that pays [up to 2% cashback](L2) in dollars or bitcoin, with the rate depending on how much a customer holds across Krak and Kraken, according to Krak's [support page](L3). That debit card is not offered in New York, Maine, Massachusetts or Indiana.

Both new credit cards need a Krakpass membership: <strong>$6.29 a month</strong> when billed annually, or free for customers with more than $3,000 a month in direct deposits. Payward has not disclosed the interest rate (APR) or other fees and says full terms will come before the cards are issued. Payward co-CEO Arjun Sethi said the card industry has spent "decades pouring resources into increasingly complex rewards schemes instead of focusing on making credit products more accessible." Krak also cited a Morning Consult survey it commissioned in July: 30% of 2,001 U.S. adults said they do not own a credit card, and 57% preferred cash rewards over points.

## How it compares

Krak is entering a field that already has crypto reward cards. The [Coinbase One Card](L4), also run with Cardless and issued by First Electronic Bank, pays up to 4% back in bitcoin based on how much a customer holds on Coinbase, requires a paid Coinbase One membership starting at $49.99 a year, and offers a USDC security deposit option starting at $100.

## What it means for beginners

A crypto rewards card is still a credit card. If you carry a balance, interest charges can quickly wipe out a 4% reward, and Krak has not said what its rate will be. Headline rates are usually "up to" figures that depend on balances, memberships or caps, so read the full terms when they are published. Rewards paid in bitcoin can lose value, and selling them later may have tax consequences.

If your main goal is simply to own some bitcoin, buying a small amount directly on a large regulated exchange such as [Coinbase](REF) is one common route. Only invest money you can afford to lose. This is not financial advice.

## What to watch next

- The APR, fees and full rewards terms Payward says it will publish before the cards are issued.
- Which U.S. states are excluded when the cards ship in early 2027.
- Whether Krak brings similar cards to other countries over the next year, as Payward plans.
