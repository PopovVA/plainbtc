Strategy, the largest corporate holder of bitcoin, kept buying last week even as the price slid to a four-month low. The company said in a [Form 8-K filing](L1) on Monday that it bought <strong>485 bitcoin</strong> for $33.4 million between Nov. 9 and Nov. 15, an average of about $68,887 per coin including fees.

That lifts its total to <strong>850,849 BTC</strong>, bought for a combined $64.20 billion at an average of <strong>$75,452 per coin</strong>. With bitcoin trading near $64,100 on Monday, the stack is worth roughly $54.5 billion, about $9.7 billion less than it cost, according to [Bitcoin Magazine](L2).

!takeaways
- Strategy bought 485 bitcoin for $33.4 million from Nov. 9 to 15, at an average of $68,887 per coin.
- It now holds 850,849 BTC, bought at an average of $75,452, which is above Monday's price near $64,000.
- Most of the cash it raised last week went to buying back its STRC preferred stock ($228.1 million), not to bitcoin.
- Strive, a smaller bitcoin treasury company, added 251 BTC and now holds 43,404 BTC.

!figure:chart

## Where the money came from

Strategy pays for most of its bitcoin by selling new shares through an "at-the-market" program, which lets a company sell stock into the market a little at a time. Last week it sold 2,087,040 shares of its Class A common stock (ticker MSTR) for $244.7 million in net proceeds, the filing shows.

Only a small slice went to bitcoin. Strategy put $33.4 million into coins, $206.3 million into buying back its STRC preferred stock and $5.0 million into cash. In total it retired 2,282,510 STRC shares for $228.1 million, covering the rest with interest earned on its cash, Bitcoin Magazine reported. The 485 coins were also less than half of the 1,036 bitcoin the company bought the week before.

STRC is a preferred share that pays a high monthly dividend. Strategy said it will keep that rate at 12% a year for December. It also used $18.9 million from its U.S. dollar reserve to pay preferred dividends during the week, leaving the reserve at $4.75 billion, [Crypto Briefing](L3) reported.

## A tough week for bitcoin and for Strategy

The purchases landed in a rough stretch for the market. Bitcoin fell to about $62,000 over the weekend, its lowest level since July, and was down about 14% over seven days, according to Crypto Briefing. Strategy's stock closed Friday at $111.62 and fell about 8.5% more in early Monday trading, the outlet said.

The company is also dealing with an index change. MSCI is removing Strategy from its MSCI USA index, and funds that track it have until Nov. 30 to sell, Bitcoin Magazine noted. Chief executive Phong Le has called the exclusion "immaterial," saying about 3% of Strategy shares are tied to MSCI benchmarks.

## Strive keeps buying too

Strategy was not the only company buying the dip. Strive (Nasdaq: ASST), which holds bitcoin as its main treasury asset, said it bought 251 BTC between Nov. 7 and Nov. 13 for about $17.5 million, an average of about $69,853 per coin. That brings its total to <strong>43,404 BTC</strong>, [TokenPost](L4) reported. Strive raised about $10 million for the purchase by selling its SATS preferred shares and has said it wants to rank second among public bitcoin holders by the end of 2026.

Together, the two companies spent about $51 million on bitcoin in their latest reporting weeks.

## What it means for beginners

Big companies buying bitcoin does not stop the price from falling. Strategy bought throughout last week, and bitcoin still dropped to a four-month low. Most of the money it raised also went to supporting its own preferred stock rather than to new coins.

The filing is a reminder that buying over time does not guarantee a profit. Strategy's average cost of $75,452 is now well above the market price, so on paper its holdings are worth less than it paid. The same can happen to anyone who buys during a rally.

If you want some exposure to bitcoin, you do not need to own shares of a company that holds it. You can buy small amounts of bitcoin directly through a regulated exchange such as [Coinbase](REF), or buy a spot bitcoin ETF in a brokerage account. Start small, only invest what you can afford to lose, and remember that prices can keep falling. This is not investment advice.

## What to watch next

- Whether Strategy's next weekly filing shows more bitcoin buying or more preferred stock buybacks.
- Index fund selling of Strategy shares ahead of MSCI's Nov. 30 deadline.
- Where bitcoin trades compared with Strategy's $75,452 average cost.
- Whether Strive keeps adding coins as it chases the number two spot among public holders.
