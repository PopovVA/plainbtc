Trump names four SEC and CFTC nominees, including a replacement for crypto advocate Hester Peirce

Published Nov 13, 2026 · PlainBTC · United States · ~5 min
Share

President Donald Trump has sent the Senate four nominations for the two agencies that oversee most of the U.S. crypto market. The White House said on Thursday, Nov. 12, that it nominated Steven Levine and Kristin Johnson to the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and Nathan Anonick and Matthew Rowland to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC).

Levine would take the SEC seat of Hester Peirce, the commissioner best known for pushing for clearer crypto rules. All four nominees need Senate confirmation, and the timing is tight: Democrats take control of the Senate in January, according to Cointelegraph.

Key takeaways

  • Trump nominated two people to the SEC and two to the CFTC, the first nominations to either agency since 2025, CoinDesk reported.
  • Steven Levine, a Republican, was picked for the seat of crypto advocate Hester Peirce, whose term expired.
  • The slate is bipartisan: Kristin Johnson and Matthew Rowland are Democrats; Levine and Nathan Anonick are Republicans.
  • The SEC has only two of its five seats filled, and the CFTC has just one: Chairman Michael Selig.

Who was nominated

According to the White House list, the four nominees are:

  • Steven Levine (SEC), for a term ending June 5, 2030, replacing Hester Peirce. Bloomberg Law reported that he has served as a senior official on the White House Council of Economic Advisers.
  • Kristin Johnson (SEC), for a term ending June 5, 2029, replacing Caroline Crenshaw. She is a former CFTC commissioner and law professor.
  • Nathan Anonick (CFTC), for a term ending June 19, 2031, replacing Rostin Behnam. He is a senior counsel on the Senate Agriculture Committee, according to Bloomberg Law.
  • Matthew Rowland (CFTC), for the rest of a term ending April 13, 2028, replacing Summer Mersinger. He also works with the Senate Agriculture Committee.

CoinDesk called the move unusual, because Trump had so far not nominated any Democrats to the SEC or CFTC in his second term.

Why Peirce's seat matters for crypto

Peirce led the SEC's Crypto Task Force and was one of the agency's most vocal supporters of clear rules for digital assets. She left the SEC in October after her term expired, Cointelegraph reported. Levine's views on crypto regulation are less publicly known.

Johnson has a longer record on the topic. During her time at the CFTC, she argued for stronger customer protections and conflict-of-interest rules for crypto firms, according to Cointelegraph. On the CFTC side, Anonick and Rowland both worked on the Clarity Act, the crypto market structure bill that stalled in the Senate in September. Selig called them "two strong bipartisan picks."

Tight timing in the Senate

The SEC nominees would normally go before the Senate Banking Committee and the CFTC nominees before the Senate Agriculture Committee. After that, the full Senate votes. The Senate is now in its lame-duck session after last week's midterm elections, and Republicans lose control of the chamber in January. If the nominations are not confirmed before then, they could face a tougher path.

Meanwhile, the agencies are moving ahead with fewer members. In an interview with CNBC published Friday, Selig said major crypto exchanges and new companies are interested in signing up under the CFTC's proposed framework for retail crypto trading, which the agency put out for comment on Oct. 5 after the Clarity Act stalled. "Those new entrants are lining up to come register with us," he said.

What it means for beginners

Nothing changes for your account today. Nominations are only the first step, and the agencies' current rules stay in place. Over time, though, the people who sit on these commissions shape how crypto exchanges, ETFs and new products are regulated in the U.S. Fuller commissions with members from both parties could change how future votes on crypto rules play out.

What to watch next

  • When the Senate Banking and Agriculture committees schedule confirmation hearings.
  • Whether the full Senate votes on the four nominees before Democrats take control in January.
  • Where Levine stands on crypto, once he speaks publicly or testifies.
  • Progress on the CFTC's proposed rules for retail crypto trading.
