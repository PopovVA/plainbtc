Bitcoin slides to $58,000 as the Fed's favorite inflation gauge hits 3.8%, on track for an eighth straight weekly loss

Published Nov 25, 2026 · PlainBTC · United States · ~5 min
Share

Bitcoin's slide picked up again on Wednesday, Nov. 25. The price fell to about $58,000 on Kraken by 8 a.m. Central time, down about 3% from Tuesday's close, after a new U.S. inflation report and a fresh drop in stock futures.

The report was the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index, the inflation gauge the Federal Reserve watches most closely. Prices rose 3.8% over the year to October, in line with forecasts but up from 3.6% in September, according to CNBC. Core PCE, which leaves out food and energy, rose 3.3%, slightly above the 3.2% economists expected.

Key takeaways

  • October PCE inflation was 3.8% year over year, nearly twice the Fed's 2% target. Core PCE was 3.3%, a little hotter than forecast.
  • Bitcoin fell to about $58,000 on Kraken on Wednesday morning, down about 25% since the end of October.
  • Bitcoin is on track for its eighth weekly loss in a row, CoinDesk reported. Only a nine-week streak in 2022 lasted longer.
  • U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost $231.4 million on Tuesday, their sixth straight day of outflows, per Farside Investors.
  • Markets fully price a quarter-point Fed rate hike at the Dec. 8 to 9 meeting, CNBC reported.

Why an inflation report moves bitcoin

When inflation runs hot, the Fed tends to keep interest rates high or raise them. Higher rates make safe assets such as Treasury bonds pay more, so riskier assets like tech stocks and crypto often lose buyers. CNBC said the market already fully prices a quarter-point rate hike when the Fed meets on Dec. 8 and 9, and Wednesday's numbers did little to change that.

The rest of the data pointed to a strong economy, which can also keep the Fed on guard. Economic growth for the third quarter was revised up to a 4.3% annual rate from 3.4%, and weekly jobless claims fell to 186,000, according to CoinDesk's live coverage. Bitcoin dipped to about $58,500 right after the 8:30 a.m. Eastern release and kept sliding from there.

Stocks and tariffs add pressure

Crypto was not falling alone. Nasdaq-100 futures were down more than 2% before the open, after South Korea's main stock index fell more than 6% on reports that the U.S. will put new tariffs on major trading partners, CoinDesk reported. Bloomberg reported that the administration was expected to formally announce a new tariff plan later on Wednesday, according to CoinDesk.

Other large coins fell further than bitcoin. On Kraken, ether traded near $1,628, solana near $66.60 and XRP near 89 cents on Wednesday morning, each down about 3% to 4% from Tuesday.

ETF investors keep pulling money

Big funds are still heading for the exit. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost a net $231.4 million on Tuesday, Nov. 24, according to Farside Investors. It was the sixth losing day in a row, though less than half of Monday's $569.7 million. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) accounted for $175.3 million. November outflows now total about $4.94 billion, already a monthly record for these funds.

Bitcoin has now lost about 25% since Oct. 31, based on Kraken daily closes. If it ends the month near this level, November would be its worst month of 2026, ahead of June. CoinDesk said it would be the worst month since June 2022, when bitcoin dropped 38%. The price is still above the 2026 low of about $55,100 it touched on Kraken on Saturday, Nov. 21, during a sharp crash and rebound.

What it means for beginners

A falling price can feel like a sale or like a warning, and nobody knows which it is in advance. Some people who believe in bitcoin over the long term buy small fixed amounts on a schedule instead of trying to guess the bottom. If you are just starting, use money you will not need soon, keep your purchases small, and check the fees on any app or exchange, such as Coinbase, before you buy. Crypto prices can keep falling for weeks. This is not financial advice.

What to watch next

  • U.S. markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early on Friday, so trading may be thin and prices jumpy.
  • The formal details of the new U.S. tariff plan, and how stocks react.
  • The Fed's Dec. 8 to 9 meeting, where a rate hike is widely expected.
  • Whether ETF outflows slow down after six straight days.
